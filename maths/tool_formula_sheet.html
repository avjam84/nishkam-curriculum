Formula & Key Reference Sheet

Every formula and key result named in the guides, in one place.

GCSE Mathematics

G1 Number

TopicFormula / key result
HCF & LCMHCF: the largest factor shared by two numbers. LCM: the smallest multiple shared by two numbers. Write each number as a product of prime factors. HCF = product of shared primes (lowest powers); LCM = product of all primes (highest powers).
Zero, Negative and Fractional Indicesa⁰ = 1, a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a, a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ. Evaluate fractional indices root first: 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4. Negative index means reciprocal, not a negative answer.
SurdsA surd is an irrational root such as √2 that cannot be simplified to an integer. √(ab) = √a × √b. Rationalise 1/√a by multiplying by √a/√a.

G2 Algebra

TopicFormula / key result
Algebraic IndicesIndex laws apply to letters: xᵃ × xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ, xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ, (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ. Deal with coefficients and letters separately: 6x⁵ ÷ 2x² = 3x³.
Expanding and FactorisingExpanding removes brackets; factorising puts them back in by taking out common factors. Factorise fully: 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3), not x(6x + 9).
Linear and Quadratic FactorisationA quadratic x² + bx + c factorises to (x + p)(x + q) where p + q = b and pq = c. Difference of two squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
SubstitutionSubstitution replaces letters with given values and evaluates. Use brackets around negative values: if x = −3, x² = (−3)² = 9.
FormulaeA formula is a rule linking variables, e.g. v = u + at. An equation is solved; a formula is used or rearranged; an identity (≡) is true for all values.

G3 Interpreting and Representing Data

TopicFormula / key result
Averages – Mean, Median, Mode, RangeMean = total ÷ number of values; median = middle value when ordered; mode = most common; range = largest − smallest. Range measures spread, not average. Choose median when there are outliers.
Frequency TablesA frequency table records how many times each value occurs. Mean from a frequency table = Σfx ÷ Σf. For grouped data use midpoints, giving an estimate.
Statistical DiagramsBar charts, pie charts, line graphs and frequency polygons represent data visually. Pie chart angle = frequency ÷ total × 360°. Frequency polygons plot frequency at class midpoints.

G4 Fractions, Ratio and Percentage

TopicFormula / key result
DecimalsRecurring decimals have digits that repeat forever, e.g. 0.3̇ = 1/3. To convert 0.4̇5̇: let x = 0.4545…, 100x = 45.45…, 99x = 45, x = 5/11.
Percentages – Reverse, Change and CompoundPercentage change = change ÷ original × 100. Compound growth: original × (multiplier)ⁿ. Reverse percentage: divide by the multiplier (e.g. price after 20% increase ÷ 1.2), never subtract 20% of the new value.
RatiosA ratio compares quantities: a : b. To share in ratio a : b, find one part = total ÷ (a + b).
Scaling up Ratio – Exchange RatesAn exchange rate is a ratio between currencies, e.g. £1 = €1.15. Multiply to convert into the currency with more units per £; divide to convert back.

G5 Angles and Trigonometry

TopicFormula / key result
Interior Angles in PolygonsThe sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon is (n − 2) × 180°. Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n.
Exterior Angles in PolygonsExterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°. For a regular polygon, n = 360° ÷ exterior angle. Interior + exterior = 180°.
PythagorasIn a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c² where c is the hypotenuse. To find a shorter side, subtract: a² = c² − b².
TrigonometrySOH CAH TOA: sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj. Label sides relative to the angle; use inverse functions to find angles. Calculator must be in degrees.

G6 Graphs

TopicFormula / key result
Linear Graphs and EquationsA straight line has equation y = mx + c, where m is the gradient and c the y-intercept. Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x. Parallel lines have equal gradients.
Quadratic GraphsThe graph of y = ax² + bx + c is a parabola. Roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis; the turning point lies on the line of symmetry.
Harder Graphs: Cubic, Reciprocal, ExponentialCubic: y = x³ shape; reciprocal: y = 1/x (asymptotes on the axes); exponential: y = kˣ. Recognise each shape by its key features: reciprocal never touches the axes; exponential passes through (0, 1) for y = kˣ.

G7 Area and Volume

TopicFormula / key result
Perimeter and AreaArea of parallelogram = base × height; trapezium = ½(a + b)h. Split compound shapes into rectangles, triangles and trapezia. Use perpendicular height.
Units and AccuracyBounds: a value rounded to the nearest unit lies within half a unit either side. Area units square the conversion: 1 m² = 10 000 cm². Volume units cube it: 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.
PrismsA prism has a constant cross-section. Volume = area of cross-section × length. Surface area = sum of areas of all faces.
CirclesCircumference C = πd = 2πr; area A = πr². Leave answers in terms of π when asked.
Sectors of CirclesA sector is a 'slice' of a circle bounded by two radii and an arc. Arc length = θ/360 × 2πr; sector area = θ/360 × πr².
Cylinders and SpheresCylinder V = πr²h; sphere V = 4/3 πr³, SA = 4πr². Curved surface of a cylinder = 2πrh.
Pyramids and ConesPyramid V = ⅓ × base area × height; cone V = ⅓πr²h, curved SA = πrl. Slant height l relates to h and r by Pythagoras: l² = r² + h².

G8 Transformation and Construction

TopicFormula / key result
Bearings and Scale DrawingsA bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, written with three figures. Back bearing = bearing ± 180°.

G9 Equations and Inequalities

TopicFormula / key result
Quadratic Equations – FactorisingSet the quadratic equal to zero, factorise, and set each bracket to zero. x² − 5x + 6 = 0 → (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 2 or 3.
Quadratic Formulax = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a for ax² + bx + c = 0. Use when the quadratic will not factorise or a decimal answer is required.
Completing the Squarex² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c. The completed square form gives the turning point (−b/2, c − (b/2)²).

G10 Probability

TopicFormula / key result
Calculating ProbabilitiesP(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total equally likely outcomes. Probabilities lie between 0 and 1. P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
Mutually Exclusive EventsEvents that cannot happen at the same time. For mutually exclusive events, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
Experimental ProbabilityRelative frequency = frequency ÷ number of trials. More trials give a more reliable estimate. Expected frequency = probability × number of trials.
Independent Events and Tree DiagramsIndependent events do not affect each other: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Multiply along branches, add between outcomes.

G11 Multiplicative Reasoning

TopicFormula / key result
Growth and Decay PercentagesRepeated percentage change: final = initial × multiplierⁿ. Growth multiplier > 1 (e.g. 1.04); decay multiplier < 1 (e.g. 0.85 for 15% depreciation).
Compound MeasuresA compound measure combines two measures, e.g. speed = distance ÷ time, density = mass ÷ volume, pressure = force ÷ area. Convert units to match before calculating; 45 minutes = 0.75 hours.
Ratio and ProportionRatio problems may give the difference between parts or one part rather than the total. If A : B = 3 : 5 and B has 12 more than A, then 2 parts = 12, so one part = 6.

G13 Trigonometry 2

TopicFormula / key result
Graphs of Sine, Cosine and Tangent Functionsy = sin x and y = cos x are periodic with period 360° and range −1 to 1; y = tan x has period 180° and asymptotes at 90°, 270°. Use the symmetry of the graphs to find all solutions in a range.
Cosine Rule with 2D/3D Shapesa² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A. Use when you know two sides and the included angle, or three sides.
Pythagoras with 2D/3D ShapesIn a cuboid, the space diagonal d satisfies d² = a² + b² + c². Find a right-angled triangle inside the 3D shape and sketch it in 2D.
Transforming Trigonometric Graphsy = f(x) + a translates vertically; y = f(x + a) translates by −a horizontally; y = −f(x) reflects in the x-axis. Apply to trig graphs: y = sin(x) + 2 shifts the wave up 2.

G14 Further Statistics

TopicFormula / key result
SamplingA sample is part of a population used to draw conclusions about the whole. Stratified sample size for a group = group size ÷ population × sample size. Random samples reduce bias.
Cumulative FrequencyCumulative frequency is a running total of frequencies. Plot at upper class boundaries. Median at n/2, quartiles at n/4 and 3n/4. IQR = UQ − LQ.
HistogramsA histogram uses frequency density = frequency ÷ class width for unequal classes. Area of a bar represents frequency.

G15 Equations and Graphs 2

TopicFormula / key result
Solving Quadratic Equations GraphicallyRoots of ax² + bx + c = 0 are the x-intercepts of y = ax² + bx + c. To solve x² − 3x = 2 from y = x² − 3x, draw y = 2 and read the intersections.
Graphs of Cubic FunctionsGraphs of y = ax³ + bx² + cx + d. Factorised form y = (x − p)(x − q)(x − r) crosses the x-axis at p, q, r.

G16 Circle Theorems

TopicFormula / key result
Angle Theorems in CirclesAngle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; angle in a semicircle = 90°; opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; alternate segment theorem. Quote the theorem as the reason for each step.
Construction – Graph of a Circlex² + y² = r² is a circle centred at the origin with radius r. Points on the circle satisfy the equation.
Equation of a TangentThe tangent at point P is perpendicular to the radius OP. Gradient of tangent = −1 ÷ gradient of OP; then use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).

G17 Algebra 2

TopicFormula / key result
FunctionsA function maps an input to one output: f(x) = 2x + 1. Composite fg(x) means do g first then f. Inverse f⁻¹ reverses f: swap x and y and rearrange.

G18 Vectors and Geometric Proof

TopicFormula / key result
Vectors and Vector NotationA vector has magnitude and direction; written as a column (x over y), as a bold letter a, or as AB with an arrow. AB = −BA.
Vector ArithmeticAdd vectors head-to-tail; multiplying by a scalar changes length, not direction (unless negative). AC = AB + BC: write a route through known vectors.
Parallel Vectors and Collinear PointsVectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other. A, B, C are collinear if AB = k × BC (parallel AND sharing point B).

G19 Proportion and Graphs

TopicFormula / key result
Direct Proportiony ∝ x means y = kx for a constant k. Also y ∝ x², y ∝ √x, etc. Find k from given values first.
Inverse Proportiony ∝ 1/x means y = k/x. As one variable doubles the other halves.
Exponential Functionsy = abˣ: growth if b > 1, decay if 0 < b < 1. Use two points to find a and b.
Translating Graphs of Functionsy = f(x) + a moves up a; y = f(x + a) moves left a. Describe as a translation by a column vector.
Reflecting and Stretching Graphs of Functionsy = −f(x) reflects in the x-axis; y = f(−x) reflects in the y-axis. Track key points such as turning points to sketch the new graph.

A level Mathematics

M12.1 Algebraic Foundations

TopicFormula / key result
QuadraticsThe discriminant b² − 4ac decides the number of real roots: >0 two, =0 one (repeated), <0 none. Completing the square gives the vertex; disguised quadratics such as x⁴ − 5x² + 4 = 0 use a substitution u = x².
Indices and Surdsa^(m/n) = ⁿ√(aᵐ); surds are rationalised using conjugates. Write expressions as powers of x (e.g. 1/√x = x^(−1/2)) ready for calculus.
Graph Transformationsy = f(x) + a, f(x + a), af(x), f(ax), −f(x), f(−x). f(ax) is a horizontal stretch scale factor 1/a. Track asymptotes and intercepts.
Co-ordinate GeometryLines: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁); circles: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r². Complete the square to find centre and radius. Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1.
The Factor TheoremIf f(a) = 0 then (x − a) is a factor of f(x). Test small integer factors of the constant term, then divide or compare coefficients.

M12.2 Trigonometry, Vectors and Calculus

TopicFormula / key result
The Binomial Theorem(a + b)ⁿ = Σ ⁿCᵣ aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ for positive integer n. ⁿCᵣ = n!/(r!(n − r)!). Keep negative signs inside brackets.
Trigonometric Functions, Equations and Identitiessin²θ + cos²θ ≡ 1 and tan θ ≡ sin θ/cos θ; sine and cosine rules; area = ½ab sin C. Find all solutions in the interval using the CAST diagram or graph symmetry. Do not divide by a trig function that may be zero.
VectorsVectors in 2D in i, j form; magnitude |a| = √(x² + y²). Direction as angle with i: tan θ = y/x. Position vectors describe points.
Differential CalculusThe derivative dy/dx is the gradient function; d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹. Differentiation from first principles: f′(x) = lim h→0 [f(x+h) − f(x)]/h. Stationary points where dy/dx = 0; second derivative classifies them.
Tangents and NormalsTangent gradient = f′(a); normal gradient = −1/f′(a). Use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
IntegrationIntegration reverses differentiation: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + c, n ≠ −1. Definite integrals give signed area; area below the x-axis is negative.
Exponentials and Logarithmslogₐ x = y ⇔ aʸ = x; d/dx(eᵏˣ) = keᵏˣ. log laws: log ab = log a + log b, log(a/b) = log a − log b, log aⁿ = n log a.

M12.3 Logarithmic Data, Kinematics and Sampling

TopicFormula / key result
Logarithmic DataIf y = axⁿ then log y = log a + n log x (a straight line); if y = abˣ then log y = log a + x log b. Read the gradient and intercept of the log graph to find the constants.
V-T & S-T GraphsOn a v–t graph, gradient = acceleration and area = displacement. On an s–t graph, gradient = velocity. Distance is total area; displacement is signed area.
SUVAT FormulaeFor constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², s = ½(u + v)t, v² = u² + 2as, s = vt − ½at². List s, u, v, a, t; pick the formula without the unknown you do not need. Take a direction as positive.
Newton's 2nd Law and its ApplicationsF = ma: the resultant force equals mass × acceleration. Draw a force diagram; resolve in the direction of motion. Weight = mg with g = 9.8 m s⁻².
Connected ParticlesParticles joined by a light inextensible string share the same acceleration and tension. Write F = ma for each particle separately, or for the whole system to find a.
Variable AccelerationWhen acceleration is not constant, v = ds/dt and a = dv/dt; s = ∫v dt. SUVAT does not apply; use calculus.
Data PresentationHistograms, box plots, cumulative frequency and measures of location and spread. Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width. Outliers are often defined as beyond 1.5 × IQR from the quartiles; use the definition given in the question.

M12.4 Statistics: Correlation to Hypothesis Testing

TopicFormula / key result
Correlation and RegressionCorrelation describes linear association; the regression line y = a + bx predicts y from x. Interpolation is reliable; extrapolation is not. Interpret b in context.
ProbabilityVenn and tree diagrams; mutually exclusive events P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B); independent events P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B). Use the addition rule P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).
Discrete DistributionsA discrete random variable takes countable values with probabilities summing to 1. Use Σ P(X = x) = 1 to find unknown constants.
The Binomial DistributionX ~ B(n, p) models the number of successes in n independent trials with constant probability p. P(X = r) = ⁿCᵣ pʳ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ʳ. State conditions in context.

M12.5 A2 Trigonometry, Partial Fractions and the Chain Rule

TopicFormula / key result
RadiansOne radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius; π radians = 180°. Calculus with trig functions requires radians.
Arcs and SectorsArc length s = rθ; sector area A = ½r²θ (θ in radians). Segment area = sector − triangle = ½r²(θ − sin θ).
Addition Formulaesin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B; cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B. Double angle formulae follow by setting A = B.
Rcos(x ± α)a cos x + b sin x can be written as R cos(x − α), where R = √(a² + b²) and tan α = b/a. Use it to solve equations and to find maximum and minimum values.
Partial FractionsSplitting a rational expression into simpler fractions. (5x + 1)/((x + 1)(x − 1)) = A/(x + 1) + B/(x − 1). Repeated factors need A/(x − a) + B/(x − a)².
The Chain Ruledy/dx = dy/du × du/dx. d/dx[(3x + 1)⁵] = 15(3x + 1)⁴.

M12.6 A2 Differentiation

TopicFormula / key result
The Product and Quotient RulesProduct: d(uv)/dx = u v′ + v u′. Quotient: d(u/v)/dx = (v u′ − u v′)/v². Define u and v clearly before differentiating.
Implicit DifferentiationDifferentiating each term with respect to x, treating y as a function of x. d/dx(y²) = 2y dy/dx; d/dx(xy) = x dy/dx + y.
Points of InflectionA point where the curve changes concavity. f″(x) = 0 and changes sign; f″ = 0 alone is not enough.
3D Vectors (summer homework)Vectors in i, j, k form; |a| = √(x² + y² + z²). Distance between points = magnitude of the difference of position vectors.

M13.1 Functions and Integration

TopicFormula / key result
Modelling with Trigonometric FunctionsReal periodic situations modelled with forms such as h = a + b sin(ωt + c). Interpret amplitude, period and maximum/minimum in context.
FunctionsDomain, range, composite and inverse functions, and modulus graphs. f⁻¹ exists only for one-to-one functions; y = f⁻¹(x) is a reflection of y = f(x) in y = x.
The Chain, Product and Quotient RulesRules for differentiating composite, product and quotient expressions (A2 extension to eˣ, ln x and trig functions). d/dx(tan x) = sec²x; d/dx(ln f(x)) = f′(x)/f(x).
Integration using the Reverse Chain RuleRecognising ∫ f′(x)[f(x)]ⁿ dx and ∫ f′(x)/f(x) dx = ln|f(x)| + c. Adjust constants by inspection, then check by differentiating.
Integration by Parts∫ u dv/dx dx = uv − ∫ v du/dx dx. Choose u to become simpler when differentiated (LATE: logs, algebra, trig, exponentials).

M13.2 Differential Equations to Numerical Methods

TopicFormula / key result
Connected Rates of ChangeLinking rates using the chain rule: dV/dt = dV/dr × dr/dt. Identify the given rate and the required rate first.
Separation of VariablesSolving dy/dx = f(x)g(y) by writing ∫ 1/g(y) dy = ∫ f(x) dx. Include + c, then use initial conditions to find it.
Parametric Equationsx and y given in terms of a parameter t. dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt). Convert to Cartesian by eliminating t.
Arithmetic and Geometric Sequences and SeriesArithmetic: uₙ = a + (n − 1)d, Sₙ = n/2[2a + (n − 1)d]. Geometric: uₙ = arⁿ⁻¹, Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), S∞ = a/(1 − r) for |r| < 1. Sigma notation and recurrence relations; identify increasing, decreasing and periodic sequences.
A2 Binomial Expansion(1 + x)ⁿ = 1 + nx + n(n−1)/2! x² + … for rational n, valid |x| < 1. Take out a factor first: (4 + x)^(1/2) = 2(1 + x/4)^(1/2), valid |x| < 4.
The Newton-Raphson Methodxₙ₊₁ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ). Fails or is slow if f′(xₙ) ≈ 0 (near a stationary point).

M13.3 A2 Mechanics and Correlation

TopicFormula / key result
MomentsMoment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. For equilibrium: resultant force = 0 and total clockwise moments = total anticlockwise moments about any point.
Inclined Planes and FrictionFriction F ≤ μR; limiting equilibrium when F = μR. On a slope at angle α, R = mg cos α (if no other perpendicular forces).
Variable Acceleration in 2DVectors r, v, a as functions of t: v = dr/dt, a = dv/dt. Differentiate/integrate each component separately.
The PMCC and Hypothesis Testing for CorrelationThe product moment correlation coefficient r measures linear correlation, −1 ≤ r ≤ 1. Test H₀: ρ = 0 by comparing r with the critical value; conclude in context.

M13.4 Probability and the Normal Distribution

TopicFormula / key result
Conditional ProbabilityP(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B). Two-way tables, Venn and tree diagrams. Independence: P(A|B) = P(A).
The Normal DistributionX ~ N(μ, σ²) is a symmetric bell-shaped continuous distribution. About 68% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ. Standardise with Z = (X − μ)/σ. Use the inverse normal to find unknown μ or σ.

A level Further Mathematics

F12.1 Complex Numbers and Matrices

TopicFormula / key result
Complex Numbersz = a + bi where i² = −1; the conjugate is z* = a − bi. Divide by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate. Non-real roots of real polynomials occur in conjugate pairs.
Argand DiagramsA plane where z = x + iy is plotted at (x, y). Modulus |z| = √(x² + y²); argument arg z is the angle from the positive real axis, −π < arg z ≤ π. Loci such as |z − a| = r are circles.
MatricesRectangular arrays; product AB is defined when columns of A = rows of B. Matrix multiplication is not commutative. det of 2×2 [a b; c d] = ad − bc; inverse = (1/det)[d −b; −c a].
Linear TransformationsA matrix maps position vectors: rotations, reflections, enlargements, stretches. The columns are images of the unit vectors. |det| is the area scale factor; (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹.

F12.2 Roots, Series and Induction

TopicFormula / key result
Roots of PolynomialsFor ax² + bx + c: α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a. For cubics: Σα = −b/a, Σαβ = c/a, αβγ = −d/a. Form new equations with transformed roots using substitution (e.g. roots 2α: replace x with x/2).
SeriesStandard results: Σr = ½n(n+1), Σr² = ⅙n(n+1)(2n+1), Σr³ = ¼n²(n+1)². Σ from r = k to n = Σ(1 to n) − Σ(1 to k−1).
Proof by InductionProve a base case, assume true for n = k, prove true for n = k + 1, conclude. Used for sums, divisibility and matrix powers. The concluding statement is required for full marks.

F12.3 Further Vectors, Volumes and Discrete Random Variables

TopicFormula / key result
Further VectorsVector equation of a line r = a + λb; scalar product a·b = |a||b|cos θ. Lines in 3D may be parallel, intersecting or skew. Perpendicular vectors have a·b = 0.
Volumes of RevolutionV = π∫y² dx about the x-axis; V = π∫x² dy about the y-axis. Deriving V = ⅓πr²h for a cone by rotating y = (r/h)x.
Discrete Random VariablesA variable whose possible values are countable, with a probability distribution. Σ P(X = x) = 1.
Expectation and Variance of a Random VariableE(X) = Σ x P(X = x); Var(X) = E(X²) − [E(X)]². E(aX + b) = aE(X) + b; Var(aX + b) = a²Var(X).

F12.4 Chi-Squared and Momentum

TopicFormula / key result
Goodness of FitTesting whether observed frequencies fit a proposed distribution. Expected frequency = total × model probability.
Chi Squared TestingX² = Σ(O − E)²/E compared with a χ² critical value. Combine classes so each E ≥ 5. Degrees of freedom = number of classes − 1 − number of estimated parameters. Contingency tables: (r − 1)(c − 1).
Impulse and Momentum in One DimensionMomentum = mv; impulse = change in momentum = Ft. Conservation of momentum in collisions: total momentum before = total after. Take a positive direction.

F12.5 Work, Energy and Collisions

TopicFormula / key result
Elastic CollisionsNewton's law of restitution: speed of separation = e × speed of approach, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1. Use with conservation of momentum to find velocities after impact. e = 1 perfectly elastic (no KE loss).
Work and PowerWork = force × distance moved in the direction of the force; power = rate of doing work = Fv. At maximum speed, driving force = resistances (acceleration zero).
Conservation of Mechanical EnergyWith no non-conservative forces, KE + GPE is constant. Work done against resistance = loss of mechanical energy (work–energy principle).

F12.6 Momentum in 2D and Oblique Collisions

TopicFormula / key result
Momentum and Impulse in 2DMomentum and impulse as vectors: I = mv − mu. Work in i and j components separately.

F13.1 Further Mechanics and Complex Numbers

TopicFormula / key result
Hooke's LawTension T = λx/l where λ is the modulus of elasticity and l the natural length. Applies to elastic strings (taut only) and springs (tension or thrust).
Elastic Potential EnergyEPE = λx²/(2l). Include EPE in energy conservation problems.
Complex NumbersA2: exponential form z = re^(iθ), de Moivre's theorem (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ, nth roots of a complex number. nth roots are equally spaced on a circle of radius r^(1/n).

F13.2 Hyperbolic Functions and Further Calculus

TopicFormula / key result
Hyperbolic Functionssinh x = (eˣ − e⁻ˣ)/2, cosh x = (eˣ + e⁻ˣ)/2, tanh x = sinh x/cosh x. cosh²x − sinh²x = 1. arsinh x = ln(x + √(x² + 1)).
Maclaurin Seriesf(x) = f(0) + f′(0)x + f″(0)x²/2! + … Standard series for eˣ, ln(1 + x) (valid −1 < x ≤ 1), sin x, cos x.
Further DifferentiationDifferentiating inverse trig and hyperbolic functions. d/dx(arcsin x) = 1/√(1 − x²); d/dx(arsinh x) = 1/√(1 + x²).
Further IntegrationIntegrals producing inverse trig/hyperbolic results; improper integrals; mean value. ∫ 1/(a² + x²) dx = (1/a) arctan(x/a) + c. Improper integrals use limits.
Polar Co-ordinatesPoints (r, θ); x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ. Area = ½∫r² dθ. Sketch curves like r = a(1 + cos θ).

F13.3 Differential Equations and Further Statistics

TopicFormula / key result
First Order Differential Equationsdy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x) solved with integrating factor e^(∫P dx). Multiply through, recognise d/dx(y × IF), integrate.
Second Order Differential Equationsa y″ + b y′ + c y = f(x): solution = complementary function + particular integral. Auxiliary equation am² + bm + c = 0: real distinct, repeated or complex roots give different CF forms.
Differential Equations in Mathematical ModellingSimple harmonic motion, damped oscillations and coupled systems. SHM: x″ = −ω²x. Damping classified as over, critical or under by the discriminant.
The Geometric and Negative Binomial DistributionsGeometric: trials until first success, P(X = x) = (1 − p)^(x−1)p. Negative binomial: trials until rth success. Geometric mean 1/p; negative binomial mean r/p.

F13.4 Probability Generating Functions and Test Quality

TopicFormula / key result
Probability Generating FunctionsG_X(t) = E(tˣ) = Σ P(X = x)tˣ. G(1) = 1; E(X) = G′(1); Var(X) = G″(1) + G′(1) − [G′(1)]². For independent X, Y: G_{X+Y}(t) = G_X(t)G_Y(t).
Quality of Hypothesis TestsType I error: rejecting a true H₀; Type II error: failing to reject a false H₀. Power = 1 − P(Type II). P(Type I) equals the actual significance level for a discrete distribution; power functions show how power changes with the parameter.
