Key definitions and results from every guide, flip to test recall.

GCSE Mathematics

G1 · Place Value

The value of a digit depends on its position: in 3.47 the 4 is worth 4 tenths.

G1 · Decimals

A decimal is a fraction with a denominator that is a power of 10.

G1 · Rounding

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby simpler value to a stated degree of accuracy.

G1 · Estimation

An estimate is a calculation done with every value rounded to 1 significant figure.

G1 · HCF & LCM

HCF: the largest factor shared by two numbers. LCM: the smallest multiple shared by two numbers.

G1 · Zero, Negative and Fractional Indices

a⁰ = 1, a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a, a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ.

G1 · Standard Form

A number written as A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer.

G1 · Surds

A surd is an irrational root such as √2 that cannot be simplified to an integer.

G1 · Combinations

The product rule for counting: if one choice has m options and another has n, there are m × n combined outcomes.

G2 · Algebraic Indices

Index laws apply to letters: xᵃ × xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ, xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ, (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ.

G2 · Expanding and Factorising

Expanding removes brackets; factorising puts them back in by taking out common factors.

G2 · Linear and Quadratic Factorisation

A quadratic x² + bx + c factorises to (x + p)(x + q) where p + q = b and pq = c.

G2 · Solving Equations

Solving finds the value of the unknown that makes the equation true.

G2 · Substitution

Substitution replaces letters with given values and evaluates.

G2 · Linear Sequences and nth Term

The nth term of a linear sequence is dn + c where d is the common difference.

G2 · Formulae

A formula is a rule linking variables, e.g. v = u + at.

G3 · Averages – Mean, Median, Mode, Range

Mean = total ÷ number of values; median = middle value when ordered; mode = most common; range = largest − smallest.

G3 · Frequency Tables

A frequency table records how many times each value occurs.

G3 · Collecting Data

Primary data is collected yourself; secondary data is collected by someone else.

G3 · Statistical Diagrams

Bar charts, pie charts, line graphs and frequency polygons represent data visually.

G3 · Time Series

A time series is a line graph of data recorded over time.

G3 · Scatter Graphs

A scatter graph plots two variables to show correlation.

G4 · Fractions

A fraction a/b represents a ÷ b.

G4 · Decimals

Recurring decimals have digits that repeat forever, e.g. 0.3̇ = 1/3.

G4 · Percentages – Reverse, Change and Compound

Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100. Compound growth: original × (multiplier)ⁿ.

G4 · Ratios

A ratio compares quantities: a : b.

G4 · Scaling up Ratio – Exchange Rates

An exchange rate is a ratio between currencies, e.g. £1 = €1.15.

G4 · Proportion

Two quantities are in direct proportion if their ratio stays constant.

G4 · Scales and Maps

A map scale 1 : n means 1 unit on the map represents n units in real life.

G5 · Angle Properties of Triangles and Quadrilaterals

Angles in a triangle sum to 180°; in a quadrilateral to 360°.

G5 · Angles in Parallel Lines

Alternate angles are equal, corresponding angles are equal, co-interior angles sum to 180°.

G5 · Interior Angles in Polygons

The sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon is (n − 2) × 180°.

G5 · Exterior Angles in Polygons

Exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°.

G5 · Pythagoras

In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c² where c is the hypotenuse.

G5 · Trigonometry

SOH CAH TOA: sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj.

G6 · Line Segments

A line segment joins two points; its midpoint is ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2).

G6 · Linear Graphs and Equations

A straight line has equation y = mx + c, where m is the gradient and c the y-intercept.

G6 · Quadratic Graphs

The graph of y = ax² + bx + c is a parabola.

G6 · Harder Graphs: Cubic, Reciprocal, Exponential

Cubic: y = x³ shape; reciprocal: y = 1/x (asymptotes on the axes); exponential: y = kˣ.

G6 · Real-Life Graphs

Graphs that model real situations, e.g. cost against units used.

G7 · Perimeter and Area

Area of parallelogram = base × height; trapezium = ½(a + b)h.

G7 · Units and Accuracy

Bounds: a value rounded to the nearest unit lies within half a unit either side.

G7 · Prisms

A prism has a constant cross-section.

G7 · Circles

Circumference C = πd = 2πr; area A = πr².

G7 · Sectors of Circles

A sector is a 'slice' of a circle bounded by two radii and an arc.

G7 · Cylinders and Spheres

Cylinder V = πr²h; sphere V = 4/3 πr³, SA = 4πr².

G7 · Pyramids and Cones

Pyramid V = ⅓ × base area × height; cone V = ⅓πr²h, curved SA = πrl.

G8 · 3D Solids – Plans and Elevations

A plan is the view from above; front and side elevations are views from the front and side.

G8 · Reflections and Rotations

A reflection needs a mirror line; a rotation needs a centre, angle and direction.

G8 · Enlargement

An enlargement needs a scale factor and centre of enlargement.

G8 · Transformation Combinations

Two transformations applied in sequence can often be described as one single transformation.

G8 · Bearings and Scale Drawings

A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, written with three figures.

G8 · Constructions

Constructions use only a ruler and compasses, e.g. perpendicular bisector and angle bisector.

G8 · Loci

A locus is the set of points satisfying a rule.

G9 · Quadratic Equations – Factorising

Set the quadratic equal to zero, factorise, and set each bracket to zero.

G9 · Quadratic Formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a for ax² + bx + c = 0.

G9 · Completing the Square

x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c.

G9 · Linear Simultaneous Equations

Two equations with two unknowns solved together by elimination or substitution.

G9 · Quadratic Simultaneous Equations

One linear and one quadratic equation, solved by substitution.

G9 · Linear Inequalities

An inequality uses <, >, ≤, ≥ to describe a range of values.

G10 · Calculating Probabilities

P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total equally likely outcomes.

G10 · Mutually Exclusive Events

Events that cannot happen at the same time.

G10 · Experimental Probability

Relative frequency = frequency ÷ number of trials.

G10 · Combined Events

Sample space diagrams list all outcomes of two events.

G10 · Independent Events and Tree Diagrams

Independent events do not affect each other: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

G10 · Conditional Probability

The probability of an event given that another has occurred.

G10 · Venn Diagrams and Set Notation

A ∪ B is union (A or B); A ∩ B is intersection (A and B); A′ is the complement.

G11 · Growth and Decay Percentages

Repeated percentage change: final = initial × multiplierⁿ.

G11 · Compound Measures

A compound measure combines two measures, e.g. speed = distance ÷ time, density = mass ÷ volume, pressure = force ÷ area.

G11 · Ratio and Proportion

Ratio problems may give the difference between parts or one part rather than the total.

G12 · Congruence

Congruent shapes are identical in shape and size.

G12 · Similarity in 2D Shapes

Similar shapes have equal angles and sides in the same ratio (scale factor k).

G12 · Similarity in 3D Shapes

Similar solids have all lengths in the same ratio.

G13 · Accuracy

Upper and lower bounds give the range a rounded value could take.

G13 · Graphs of Sine, Cosine and Tangent Functions

y = sin x and y = cos x are periodic with period 360° and range −1 to 1; y = tan x has period 180° and asymptotes at 90°, 270°.

G13 · Cosine Rule with 2D/3D Shapes

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A.

G13 · Pythagoras with 2D/3D Shapes

In a cuboid, the space diagonal d satisfies d² = a² + b² + c².

G13 · Transforming Trigonometric Graphs

y = f(x) + a translates vertically; y = f(x + a) translates by −a horizontally; y = −f(x) reflects in the x-axis.

G14 · Sampling

A sample is part of a population used to draw conclusions about the whole.

G14 · Cumulative Frequency

Cumulative frequency is a running total of frequencies.

G14 · Stem and Leaf

A diagram that orders data while keeping the raw values.

G14 · Box Plots

A box plot shows minimum, LQ, median, UQ and maximum.

G14 · Histograms

A histogram uses frequency density = frequency ÷ class width for unequal classes.

G15 · Solving Simultaneous Equations Graphically

The solution is the point(s) where the graphs intersect.

G15 · Representing Inequalities Graphically

A region on a graph satisfying one or more inequalities.

G15 · Solving Quadratic Equations Graphically

Roots of ax² + bx + c = 0 are the x-intercepts of y = ax² + bx + c.

G15 · Graphs of Quadratic Functions

Parabolas with a turning point; ∪-shaped if a > 0, ∩-shaped if a < 0.

G15 · Graphs of Cubic Functions

Graphs of y = ax³ + bx² + cx + d.

G16 · Radii and Chords

The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

G16 · Tangents

A tangent meets the radius at 90°; tangents from an external point are equal in length.

G16 · Angle Theorems in Circles

Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; angle in a semicircle = 90°; opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; alternate segment theorem.

G16 · Applying Circle Theorems

Multi-step problems combine circle theorems with angle facts.

G16 · Construction – Graph of a Circle

x² + y² = r² is a circle centred at the origin with radius r.

G16 · Equation of a Tangent

The tangent at point P is perpendicular to the radius OP.

G17 · Rearranging Formulae

Changing the subject so a different variable is isolated.

G17 · Algebraic Fractions

Fractions with algebraic expressions in the numerator and/or denominator.

G17 · Simplifying Algebraic Fractions

Cancel common factors, not common terms.

G17 · Surds

Expanding and rationalising surd expressions.

G17 · Solving Algebraic Fraction Equations

Equations with unknowns in denominators.

G17 · Functions

A function maps an input to one output: f(x) = 2x + 1.

G18 · Vectors and Vector Notation

A vector has magnitude and direction; written as a column (x over y), as a bold letter a, or as AB with an arrow.

G18 · Vector Arithmetic

Add vectors head-to-tail; multiplying by a scalar changes length, not direction (unless negative).

G18 · Parallel Vectors and Collinear Points

Vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other.

G18 · Solving Geometric Problems

Use vector routes to prove geometric results.

G19 · Direct Proportion

y ∝ x means y = kx for a constant k.

G19 · Inverse Proportion

y ∝ 1/x means y = k/x.

G19 · Functions

Function notation f(x); domain is the input set.

G19 · Exponential Functions

y = abˣ: growth if b > 1, decay if 0 < b < 1.

G19 · Non-linear Graphs

Graphs that are not straight lines, including circles, cubics, reciprocals and exponentials.

G19 · Translating Graphs of Functions

y = f(x) + a moves up a; y = f(x + a) moves left a.

G19 · Reflecting and Stretching Graphs of Functions

y = −f(x) reflects in the x-axis; y = f(−x) reflects in the y-axis.

A level Mathematics

M12.1 · Quadratics

The discriminant b² − 4ac decides the number of real roots: >0 two, =0 one (repeated), <0 none.

M12.1 · Indices and Surds

a^(m/n) = ⁿ√(aᵐ); surds are rationalised using conjugates.

M12.1 · Simultaneous Equations and Inequalities

Solve linear–quadratic systems by substitution; solve quadratic inequalities with a sketch.

M12.1 · Graph Transformations

y = f(x) + a, f(x + a), af(x), f(ax), −f(x), f(−x).

M12.1 · Co-ordinate Geometry

Lines: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁); circles: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r².

M12.1 · The Factor Theorem

If f(a) = 0 then (x − a) is a factor of f(x).

M12.1 · Further Algebra

Algebraic division and proof (deduction, exhaustion, counter-example).

M12.2 · The Binomial Theorem

(a + b)ⁿ = Σ ⁿCᵣ aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ for positive integer n.

M12.2 · Trigonometric Functions, Equations and Identities

sin²θ + cos²θ ≡ 1 and tan θ ≡ sin θ/cos θ; sine and cosine rules; area = ½ab sin C.

M12.2 · Vectors

Vectors in 2D in i, j form; magnitude |a| = √(x² + y²).

M12.2 · Differential Calculus

The derivative dy/dx is the gradient function; d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹.

M12.2 · Tangents and Normals

Tangent gradient = f′(a); normal gradient = −1/f′(a).

M12.2 · Integration

Integration reverses differentiation: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + c, n ≠ −1.

M12.2 · Exponentials and Logarithms

logₐ x = y ⇔ aʸ = x; d/dx(eᵏˣ) = keᵏˣ.

M12.3 · Logarithmic Data

If y = axⁿ then log y = log a + n log x (a straight line); if y = abˣ then log y = log a + x log b.

M12.3 · V-T & S-T Graphs

On a v–t graph, gradient = acceleration and area = displacement. On an s–t graph, gradient = velocity.

M12.3 · SUVAT Formulae

For constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², s = ½(u + v)t, v² = u² + 2as, s = vt − ½at².

M12.3 · Newton's 2nd Law and its Applications

F = ma: the resultant force equals mass × acceleration.

M12.3 · Connected Particles

Particles joined by a light inextensible string share the same acceleration and tension.

M12.3 · Variable Acceleration

When acceleration is not constant, v = ds/dt and a = dv/dt; s = ∫v dt.

M12.3 · Sampling

A sample is a subset of the population; methods include simple random, systematic, stratified, quota, opportunity.

M12.3 · Data Presentation

Histograms, box plots, cumulative frequency and measures of location and spread.

M12.4 · Correlation and Regression

Correlation describes linear association; the regression line y = a + bx predicts y from x.

M12.4 · Probability

Venn and tree diagrams; mutually exclusive events P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B); independent events P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B).

M12.4 · Discrete Distributions

A discrete random variable takes countable values with probabilities summing to 1.

M12.4 · The Binomial Distribution

X ~ B(n, p) models the number of successes in n independent trials with constant probability p.

M12.4 · Hypothesis Testing

A test of H₀ against H₁ using a significance level.

M12.5 · Radians

One radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius; π radians = 180°.

M12.5 · Arcs and Sectors

Arc length s = rθ; sector area A = ½r²θ (θ in radians).

M12.5 · Small Angle Approximations

For small θ in radians: sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ, cos θ ≈ 1 − θ²/2.

M12.5 · Addition Formulae

sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B; cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B.

M12.5 · Rcos(x ± α)

a cos x + b sin x can be written as R cos(x − α), where R = √(a² + b²) and tan α = b/a.

M12.5 · Partial Fractions

Splitting a rational expression into simpler fractions.

M12.5 · The Chain Rule

dy/dx = dy/du × du/dx.

M12.6 · The Product and Quotient Rules

Product: d(uv)/dx = u v′ + v u′. Quotient: d(u/v)/dx = (v u′ − u v′)/v².

M12.6 · Implicit Differentiation

Differentiating each term with respect to x, treating y as a function of x.

M12.6 · Concave and Convex Functions

f is convex where f″(x) > 0 and concave where f″(x) < 0.

M12.6 · Points of Inflection

A point where the curve changes concavity.

M12.6 · Trapezium Rule (summer homework)

∫ y dx ≈ ½h[y₀ + 2(y₁ + … + yₙ₋₁) + yₙ].

M12.6 · 3D Vectors (summer homework)

Vectors in i, j, k form; |a| = √(x² + y² + z²).

M13.1 · Modelling with Trigonometric Functions

Real periodic situations modelled with forms such as h = a + b sin(ωt + c).

M13.1 · Functions

Domain, range, composite and inverse functions, and modulus graphs.

M13.1 · The Chain, Product and Quotient Rules

Rules for differentiating composite, product and quotient expressions (A2 extension to eˣ, ln x and trig functions).

M13.1 · Integration using the Reverse Chain Rule

Recognising ∫ f′(x)[f(x)]ⁿ dx and ∫ f′(x)/f(x) dx = ln|f(x)| + c.

M13.1 · Integration by Substitution

Replace a variable with u to simplify an integral.

M13.1 · Integration by Parts

∫ u dv/dx dx = uv − ∫ v du/dx dx.

M13.2 · Connected Rates of Change

Linking rates using the chain rule: dV/dt = dV/dr × dr/dt.

M13.2 · Separation of Variables

Solving dy/dx = f(x)g(y) by writing ∫ 1/g(y) dy = ∫ f(x) dx.

M13.2 · Parametric Equations

x and y given in terms of a parameter t.

M13.2 · Arithmetic and Geometric Sequences and Series

Arithmetic: uₙ = a + (n − 1)d, Sₙ = n/2[2a + (n − 1)d]. Geometric: uₙ = arⁿ⁻¹, Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), S∞ = a/(1 − r) for |r| < 1.

M13.2 · A2 Binomial Expansion

(1 + x)ⁿ = 1 + nx + n(n−1)/2! x² + … for rational n, valid |x| < 1.

M13.2 · The Newton-Raphson Method

xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ).

M13.2 · Proof by Contradiction

Assume the opposite and derive a contradiction.

M13.3 · Moments

Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot.

M13.3 · Resolving Forces at Angles

Split a force F at angle θ into F cos θ and F sin θ components.

M13.3 · Inclined Planes and Friction

Friction F ≤ μR; limiting equilibrium when F = μR.

M13.3 · Projectiles

Motion under gravity with horizontal and vertical components treated separately.

M13.3 · Variable Acceleration in 2D

Vectors r, v, a as functions of t: v = dr/dt, a = dv/dt.

M13.3 · The PMCC and Hypothesis Testing for Correlation

The product moment correlation coefficient r measures linear correlation, −1 ≤ r ≤ 1.

M13.4 · Conditional Probability

P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B).

M13.4 · Modelling with Probability

Using probability models and critiquing their assumptions.

M13.4 · The Normal Distribution

X ~ N(μ, σ²) is a symmetric bell-shaped continuous distribution.

M13.4 · The Sample Mean Distribution

For X ~ N(μ, σ²), the sample mean X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n).

M13.4 · A2 Hypothesis Testing

Testing the mean of a normal distribution using X̄.

A level Further Mathematics

F12.1 · Complex Numbers

z = a + bi where i² = −1; the conjugate is z* = a − bi.

F12.1 · Argand Diagrams

A plane where z = x + iy is plotted at (x, y).

F12.1 · Matrices

Rectangular arrays; product AB is defined when columns of A = rows of B.

F12.1 · Linear Transformations

A matrix maps position vectors: rotations, reflections, enlargements, stretches.

F12.2 · Roots of Polynomials

For ax² + bx + c: α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a. For cubics: Σα = −b/a, Σαβ = c/a, αβγ = −d/a.

F12.2 · Series

Standard results: Σr = ½n(n+1), Σr² = ⅙n(n+1)(2n+1), Σr³ = ¼n²(n+1)².

F12.2 · Proof by Induction

Prove a base case, assume true for n = k, prove true for n = k + 1, conclude.

F12.3 · Further Vectors

Vector equation of a line r = a + λb; scalar product a·b = |a||b|cos θ.

F12.3 · Volumes of Revolution

V = π∫y² dx about the x-axis; V = π∫x² dy about the y-axis.

F12.3 · Discrete Random Variables

A variable whose possible values are countable, with a probability distribution.

F12.3 · Expectation and Variance of a Random Variable

E(X) = Σ x P(X = x); Var(X) = E(X²) − [E(X)]².

F12.4 · Goodness of Fit

Testing whether observed frequencies fit a proposed distribution.

F12.4 · Chi Squared Testing

X² = Σ(O − E)²/E compared with a χ² critical value.

F12.4 · Impulse and Momentum in One Dimension

Momentum = mv; impulse = change in momentum = Ft.

F12.5 · Elastic Collisions

Newton's law of restitution: speed of separation = e × speed of approach, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1.

F12.5 · Work and Power

Work = force × distance moved in the direction of the force; power = rate of doing work = Fv.

F12.5 · Conservation of Mechanical Energy

With no non-conservative forces, KE + GPE is constant.

F12.6 · Momentum and Impulse in 2D

Momentum and impulse as vectors: I = mv − mu.

F12.6 · Oblique Collisions between a Particle and One or More Walls

At a smooth wall, the velocity component parallel to the wall is unchanged; the perpendicular component reverses and is multiplied by e.

F13.1 · Oblique Collisions in 2D

Collisions between two smooth spheres: momentum conserved along the line of centres; perpendicular components unchanged.

F13.1 · Hooke's Law

Tension T = λx/l where λ is the modulus of elasticity and l the natural length.

F13.1 · Elastic Potential Energy

EPE = λx²/(2l).

F13.1 · Complex Numbers

A2: exponential form z = re^(iθ), de Moivre's theorem (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ, nth roots of a complex number.

F13.1 · Method of Differences

Summing a series by writing each term as f(r) − f(r + 1) so terms cancel.

F13.2 · Hyperbolic Functions

sinh x = (eˣ − e⁻ˣ)/2, cosh x = (eˣ + e⁻ˣ)/2, tanh x = sinh x/cosh x.

F13.2 · Maclaurin Series

f(x) = f(0) + f′(0)x + f″(0)x²/2! + …

F13.2 · Further Differentiation

Differentiating inverse trig and hyperbolic functions.

F13.2 · Further Integration

Integrals producing inverse trig/hyperbolic results; improper integrals; mean value.

F13.2 · Polar Co-ordinates

Points (r, θ); x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ.

F13.3 · First Order Differential Equations

dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x) solved with integrating factor e^(∫P dx).

F13.3 · Second Order Differential Equations

a y″ + b y′ + c y = f(x): solution = complementary function + particular integral.

F13.3 · Differential Equations in Mathematical Modelling

Simple harmonic motion, damped oscillations and coupled systems.

F13.3 · The Geometric and Negative Binomial Distributions

Geometric: trials until first success, P(X = x) = (1 − p)^(x−1)p. Negative binomial: trials until rth success.

F13.3 · Further Hypothesis Testing

Hypothesis tests using the geometric and other distributions, including critical regions.

F13.3 · The Central Limit Theorem

For large n, X̄ is approximately N(μ, σ²/n) whatever the population distribution.

F13.4 · Probability Generating Functions

G_X(t) = E(tˣ) = Σ P(X = x)tˣ.

F13.4 · Quality of Hypothesis Tests

Type I error: rejecting a true H₀; Type II error: failing to reject a false H₀. Power = 1 − P(Type II).
