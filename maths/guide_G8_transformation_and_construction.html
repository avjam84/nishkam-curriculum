G8.1 3D Solids – Plans and Elevations

Key definitionA plan is the view from above; front and side elevations are views from the front and side.

Core knowledge. Draw elevations to scale on squared paper with no perspective. This builds directly on the definition above: learn the definition so you can quote it, then practise the technique until it is automatic, because multi-step questions combine 3d solids – plans and elevations with other topics in this block.

4. Common Mistakes

  1. Describing a transformation without all required information. Why it is wrong: Two transformations applied in sequence can often be described as one single transformation. Perform them carefully in order, then identify the single equivalent.
  2. Measuring bearings anticlockwise or not from North. Why it is wrong: A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, written with three figures. Back bearing = bearing ± 180°.
  3. Writing bearing 45° instead of 045°. Why it is wrong: A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, written with three figures. Back bearing = bearing ± 180°.
  4. Rubbing out construction arcs. Why it is wrong: Constructions use only a ruler and compasses, e.g. perpendicular bisector and angle bisector. Leave construction arcs visible — they earn the marks.
  5. Confusing negative scale factor with reduction. Why it is wrong: An enlargement needs a scale factor and centre of enlargement. Scale factor between 0 and 1 makes it smaller; a negative scale factor puts the image on the opposite side of the centre, inverted.

6. Colour-Coded Model Answers

To be confirmed by teacherThe assessment objectives AO1–AO3 and their colour-coding, and the mark allocations shown on model answers, are national GCSE/A level Mathematics conventions, not from the curriculum map.
AO1 Use and apply standard techniquesAO2 Reason, interpret and communicate mathematicallyAO3 Solve problems within mathematics and in other contexts

Question type 1: Calculate / Find 2 marks

The bearing of B from A is 070°. Find the bearing of A from B.

Show model answer
AO1 — Back bearing = 070° + 180°.
AO2 — = 250°, which is less than 360° so no adjustment is needed. The bearing of A from B is 250°.
What makes this workEach step is written on its own line, so method marks (AO1) are earned before the final answer. The last step checks or interprets the result (AO2), which is where full marks are usually secured. The chain of reasoning uses connectives such as "therefore", "which means" and "this shows that".

Question type 2: Show that / Prove 2 marks

Show clearly that the answer to the question above follows from the given information: = 250°, which is less than 360° so no adjustment is needed. The bearing of A from B is 250°.

Show model answer
AO2 — State what is given and what must be shown, so the examiner can follow the argument.
AO1 — Back bearing = 070° + 180°.
AO2 — = 250°, which is less than 360° so no adjustment is needed. The bearing of A from B is 250°.
AO2 — Concluding statement: this shows that the required result holds, as given.
What makes this workIn a "show that" question the answer is given, so the marks are for the working. No step is skipped, the argument starts from the given facts, and it ends with an explicit concluding sentence.

Question type 3: Explain / Interpret 2 marks

A student made this error: "Describing a transformation without all required information." Explain why the student is wrong and state the correct approach.

Show model answer
AO2 — The student is wrong because the correct rule is: Two transformations applied in sequence can often be described as one single transformation.
AO1 — Correct approach: Perform them carefully in order, then identify the single equivalent.
What makes this workThe answer names the error, gives the correct rule as the reason ("because"), and then states the correct method. Explanations without a reason score at most one mark.

Evaluation

9. Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

In mathematics, "evaluation" means justifying, checking and commenting on answers. Adapt these sentences:

  1. This answer is reasonable because an estimate using values rounded to 1 significant figure gives a result of the same size.
  2. Since the rule for 3d solids – plans and elevations has been applied correctly at each step, the final result follows.
  3. This method is more efficient than the alternative because it reaches the answer in fewer steps with less chance of arithmetic error.
  4. The answer is only an approximation, because values were rounded during the calculation; working to full accuracy would give a more reliable result.
  5. The model assumes conditions that may not hold in reality, so the answer should be treated as an estimate rather than an exact value.
  6. Substituting the answer back into the original problem confirms it, which shows that no error has been made.
  7. Using loci is appropriate here because the question gives exactly the information that this technique requires.
Reminders

10. Exam Reminders

To be confirmed by teacherReminders about marks, accuracy conventions and formula sheets are general exam conventions, not from the curriculum map; check them against the confirmed exam board.
  1. Rotation: centre, angle, direction.
  2. Enlargement: scale factor and centre.
  3. Bearings: from North, clockwise, three figures.
  4. Show construction arcs.
  5. Read the command word: 'show that' needs every step; 'write down' needs no working.
  6. Give answers to the accuracy requested; if none is stated, use 3 significant figures for non-exact answers.
  7. Never round in the middle of a calculation; keep full calculator values until the end.
  8. If you get stuck, write down the formula or the first step anyway: method marks are independent of the final answer.
