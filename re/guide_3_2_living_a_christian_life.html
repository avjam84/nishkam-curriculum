Concepts

3.2.1 Christian Worship

Key definitionLiturgical and non-liturgical worship.

Liturgical worship follows a set order (e.g. Catholic Mass); non-liturgical worship is freer (e.g. Pentecostal, Quaker silence).

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on christian worship; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on christian worship: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.

3.2.2 The Sacraments

Key definitionSacrament: an outward sign of inward grace.

Catholic and Orthodox Christians recognise seven sacraments; most Protestants recognise two (baptism and Eucharist).

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on the sacraments; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.

Diagrams

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing instead of explaining. Listing facts about christian worship without saying why they matter earns AO1 only.
  2. Treating believers as one group. Questions often ask for divergent views; say that views differ and why.
  3. Vague definitions. Use the precise key term (e.g. "Liturgical and non-liturgical worship.") rather than everyday language.
  4. No source of wisdom in (c) answers. The 5-mark question requires a named source of wisdom and authority; without one the answer is capped.
  5. Unsupported judgements. A conclusion must follow from the arguments given, not appear from nowhere.
Examiner trapDo not move between topics: an answer on the worldwide church must stay on that issue.

Example question stems

  • Outline three Christian beliefs about christian worship. (3)
  • Explain two reasons why the sacraments is important to Christians. (4)
  • Explain two Christian teachings about prayer. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)
  • 'The worldwide Church is the most important issue in this topic.' Evaluate this statement. (12)
Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationSource / scholarconnective

Explain question4 marks

Liturgical and non-liturgical worship. this means that Liturgical worship follows a set order (e.g.

Sacrament: an outward sign of inward grace. this is because Catholic and Orthodox Christians recognise seven sacraments; most Protestants recognise two (baptism and Eucharist)..

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective; no irrelevant material.
Evaluative answer: FOR → AGAINST → WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT12 marks

Statement: "Christian Worship matters more than the sacraments."

FOR: Liturgical worship follows a set order (e.g. Catholic Mass); non-liturgical worship is freer (e.g. Pentecostal, Quaker silence). this shows that christian worship shapes how Christian believers understand everything else in the topic.

AGAINST: Catholic and Orthodox Christians recognise seven sacraments; most Protestants recognise two (baptism and Eucharist). However, without the sacraments belief has little practical effect, which could lead to a faith that is only theoretical.

JUDGEMENT: Overall, the two are interdependent; therefore the statement is only partly convincing, because belief about christian worship gives the reason for the sacraments.

Why this is top levelLogical chains of reasoning on both sides, accurate knowledge, and a judgement that follows from the arguments. Add a named source of wisdom to strengthen AO1.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • "The stronger argument is … because it rests on a clearer source of authority."
  • "This view is persuasive to a believer, but less so to a non-religious thinker, because …"
  • "Although christian worship is central, its importance depends on …"
  • "The weakness of this argument is that it assumes …"
  • "On balance, the evidence suggests …, so the statement is largely / partly / not convincing."
  • "Divergent views show there is no single answer; however, the most widely held view is …"
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn a precise definition for each of the 8 sub-topics on this page.
  2. Have at least one source of wisdom and authority ready for every sub-topic.
  3. Develop every point with a connective ("this means that", "therefore").
  4. Always show divergent views where they exist.
  5. End evaluative answers with a judgement that follows from your reasoning.
