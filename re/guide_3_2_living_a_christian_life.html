Concepts

3.2.1 Christian Worship

Key definitionLiturgical and non-liturgical worship.

Liturgical worship follows a set order (e.g. Catholic Mass); non-liturgical worship is freer (e.g. Pentecostal, Quaker silence). Worship brings Christians together as the body of Christ and expresses praise and thanks to God.

Common mistakeSaying all Christians worship the same way.
Evaluation pointIs liturgical or informal worship better? Both serve different needs.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about christian worship; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.2 The Sacraments

Key definitionSacrament: an outward sign of inward grace.

Catholic and Orthodox Christians recognise seven sacraments; most Protestants recognise two (baptism and Eucharist). Baptism welcomes a person into the church; the Eucharist remembers Jesus' death and resurrection.

Common mistakeWrong count of sacraments.
Evaluation pointDo sacraments need to be physical? Quakers say grace is inward.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the sacraments; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.3 Prayer

Key definitionSet prayer and informal prayer.

Set prayers include the Lord's Prayer; informal prayers are in the person's own words. Both build a relationship with God. Many Christians use both types: set prayers in services and informal prayers at home.

Common mistakeTreating set prayer as insincere.
Evaluation pointIs set or informal prayer more meaningful? Both can be sincere.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about prayer; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.4 Pilgrimage

Key definitionPilgrimage: a journey to a holy place.

Examples include Lourdes and Iona; pilgrimage may bring healing, reflection or a sense of community. Many pilgrims describe returning with renewed faith rather than physical change.

Common mistakeTreating pilgrimage as a holiday.
Evaluation pointIs pilgrimage necessary? Not required in Christianity, but valued.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about pilgrimage; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.5 Christian Celebrations

Key definitionFestivals such as Christmas and Easter.

Christmas celebrates the incarnation; Easter celebrates the resurrection and is seen as the most important festival. Festivals combine worship, family and community activities.

Common mistakeMaking Christmas the most important festival.
Evaluation pointHas commercialisation weakened festivals? Many Christians think so.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about christian celebrations; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.6 The future of the church

Key definitionMission and evangelism: spreading the Christian message.

The church seeks to grow through mission, evangelism and church planting. Churches also adapt to changes in society, for example through online worship.

Common mistakeIgnoring mission and evangelism.
Evaluation pointShould Christians evangelise in a multi-faith society? Views differ.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the future of the church; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.7 The local Church

Key definitionThe local parish or congregation.

The local church provides worship, rites of passage and community support such as food banks. Many churches run youth groups, parent and toddler groups and support for the homeless.

Common mistakeNo examples of local work.
Evaluation pointIs social action as important as worship? Many argue faith without works is dead.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the local church; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.2.8 The worldwide Church

Key definitionThe global Christian community.

The worldwide church works for reconciliation and supports persecuted Christians and the poor through charities such as Christian Aid and CAFOD. Churches also work together through ecumenical bodies to promote unity.

Common mistakeGeneric answer with no named charity.
Evaluation pointShould the church focus locally or globally? Both are part of mission.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the worldwide church; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Sacrament numbers. Catholic/Orthodox seven; most Protestants two.
  2. Pilgrimage compulsory. It is not.
  3. Christmas most important. Easter is usually seen as most important.
  4. No named organisations. Use Christian Aid, CAFOD, Trussell.
  5. Missing divergence. Show different denominations.

Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationconnective

Outline three Christian beliefs about christian worship. (3)3 marks
  1. Liturgical and non-liturgical worship.
  2. Sacrament: an outward sign of inward grace.
  3. Set prayer and informal prayer.
What makes this workThree separate, accurate points; no development is needed, which saves time for the 12-mark question.
Explain two reasons why pilgrimage is important to Christians. (4)4 marks

Pilgrimage allows Christians to feel closer to God in a holy place; this means that their faith may be strengthened.

Some pilgrims seek healing, as at Lourdes; as a result pilgrimage can bring hope even without a cure.

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective that explains why it matters.
Explain two reasons why the sacraments are important to Christians. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)5 marks

Sacraments are believed to give God's grace; this means that they strengthen a Christian's relationship with God.

The Eucharist follows Jesus' instruction at the Last Supper to 'do this in remembrance of me', which shows that it is commanded by Jesus himself.

Source of wisdom and authority: Last Supper account: exact Gospel reference to be supplied by teacher

What makes this workTwo developed points plus a named source of wisdom and authority that directly supports one of them. The exact source wording is marked for teacher confirmation.
Evaluate: 'The most important role of the church is helping the poor.' (12)12 marks

FOR (1): Jesus taught in Matthew 25 that serving the needy is serving him; this shows that social action is central.

FOR (2): The early church shared possessions to help those in need, which shows that care for the poor has always been part of Christian life.

AGAINST (1): However, worship and the sacraments are the church's unique role, which could lead to the view that charity can be done by anyone.

AGAINST (2): Some Christians believe preaching the gospel is the church's first task; this is because salvation is seen as more important than material help.

WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT: Overall, worship motivates service; therefore both are essential and the statement is overstated.

What makes this workTwo developed points on each side, each a chain of reasoning with highlighted connectives; the judgement weighs the sides and explains why one is stronger rather than simply repeating them. Adding a source of wisdom and authority on each side would strengthen it further.

Real-World Examples

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • Different forms of worship suit different Christians, so it is not convincing to call one form better than another.
  • Sacraments matter to most Christians, though Quakers show that some believe grace can be received without outward signs.
  • The local church is arguably more effective than the worldwide church, because it meets needs directly in its community.
  • Mission remains central to Christianity, but in a multi-faith society many Christians emphasise dialogue alongside evangelism.
  • Easter is more important than Christmas, because the resurrection is the basis of Christian hope.
  • Overall, worship and social action are inseparable, since worship motivates Christians to serve others.
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Liturgical vs non-liturgical.
  2. Seven vs two sacraments.
  3. Lord's Prayer as model.
  4. Lourdes and Iona.
  5. Easter most important.
  6. Name a local and a worldwide church example.
  7. Spend roughly a minute per mark, so leave most time for the 12-mark (d) question.
  8. In (c) answers, name the source precisely and explain how it supports the point.
  9. In (d) answers, give arguments on both sides, include a non-religious or divergent view where relevant, and finish with a justified conclusion.
