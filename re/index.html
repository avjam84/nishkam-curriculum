GCSE Paper 1 – Religion and Ethics through the Sikh Faith

1.1

Sikh Beliefs

The nature of God, creation, human life, life after death, purpose of life, oneness of humanity, sewa and sangat.

Open →

1.2

Living the Religious Life

Features of the gurdwara, langar, nam japna, prayer and the Guru Granth Sahib, Akhand Path, gurpurbs and commemorations, birth and naming ceremonies.

Open →

1.3

Marriage and the Family

Purpose of marriage, sexual relationships, family life, community support, contraception, divorce, equality of men and women, divergent teachings on gender prejudice.

Open →

1.4

Matters of Life and Death

Origins of the world, sanctity of life, scientific and non-religious explanations, abortion, death and the afterlife, non-religious arguments against afterlife, euthanasia, the natural world.

Ethos

Our curriculum pillars

Faith-Inspired

Learning from the wisdom of all traditions. RE studies Sikh and Christian belief alongside philosophical and non-religious views, so students learn from as well as about faith.

Virtues-Led

Fostering compassion, humility, justice and courage. Ethical topics such as equality, life and death and social justice ask students to reason fairly and listen to views unlike their own.

Aspiring for Excellence

Becoming the best human beings we can possibly be. Precise knowledge, careful argument and well-supported judgement are the standard in every answer.

Nishkam School Trust mission

"To empower children with virtues that enable them to excel academically and spiritually, inspiring them to serve humanity selflessly — with an abundance of love, compassion and forgiveness."

Virtues

Three Virtues

Peacefulness

In RE, peacefulness means discussing deeply held beliefs calmly, especially in debates on abortion, euthanasia or the problem of evil, where people disagree strongly.

Diligence

Diligence means learning key terms and sources of wisdom accurately and revisiting them through regular retrieval practice.

Determination

Determination means redrafting 12-mark answers and A level essays until the reasoning is sustained and the judgement is justified.
