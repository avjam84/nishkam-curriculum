Concepts

A1.1 An introduction to Philosophical thinking at A level

Key definitionDeductive / inductive and a priori / a posteriori arguments.

Students must distinguish argument types and evaluate premises and conclusions.

Exam linkBe ready to explain an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level accurately and to evaluate it against scholarly views in extended writing.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.

A1.2 Arguments for the existence of God

Key definitionArguments for God: rational cases for theism.

These include design, cosmological and ontological arguments, each with strengths and criticisms.

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing instead of explaining. Listing facts about an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level without saying why they matter earns AO1 only.
  2. Treating believers as one group. Questions often ask for divergent views; say that views differ and why.
  3. Vague definitions. Use the precise key term (e.g. "Deductive / inductive and a priori / a posteriori arguments.") rather than everyday language.
  4. Narrating scholars without evaluating them. A level essays reward critical analysis, not summaries of who said what.
  5. Unsupported judgements. A conclusion must follow from the arguments given, not appear from nowhere.
Examiner trapDo not move between topics: an answer on language games must stay on that issue.

Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationSource / scholarconnective

Explain-style answerTariff TBC

Deductive / inductive and a priori / a posteriori arguments. this means that Students must distinguish argument types and evaluate premises and conclusions..

Arguments for God: rational cases for theism. this is because These include design, cosmological and ontological arguments, each with strengths and criticisms..

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective; no irrelevant material.
Evaluative answer: FOR → AGAINST → WEIGHTED JUDGEMENTTariff TBC

Statement: "An introduction to Philosophical thinking at A level matters more than arguments for the existence of god."

FOR: Students must distinguish argument types and evaluate premises and conclusions. this shows that an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level shapes how scholarly believers understand everything else in the topic.

AGAINST: These include design, cosmological and ontological arguments, each with strengths and criticisms. However, without arguments for the existence of god belief has little practical effect, which could lead to a faith that is only theoretical.

JUDGEMENT: Overall, the two are interdependent; therefore the statement is only partly convincing, because belief about an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level gives the reason for arguments for the existence of god.

Why this is top levelLogical chains of reasoning on both sides, accurate knowledge, and a judgement that follows from the arguments.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • "The stronger argument is … because it rests on a clearer source of authority."
  • "This view is persuasive to a believer, but less so to a non-religious thinker, because …"
  • "Although an introduction to philosophical thinking at a level is central, its importance depends on …"
  • "The weakness of this argument is that it assumes …"
  • "On balance, the evidence suggests …, so the statement is largely / partly / not convincing."
  • "Divergent views show there is no single answer; however, the most widely held view is …"
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn a precise definition for each of the 11 sub-topics on this page.
  2. Know at least one scholar or argument for and against each sub-topic.
  3. Develop every point with a connective ("this means that", "therefore").
  4. Always show divergent views where they exist.
  5. End evaluative answers with a judgement that follows from your reasoning.
