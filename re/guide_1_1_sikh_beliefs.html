Concepts

1.1.1 The nature of God

Key definitionIk Onkar: there is one God, the opening of the Mool Mantar.

The Mool Mantar describes God as one, eternal truth (Sat Nam), creator (Karta Purakh), without fear (Nirbhau) and without hatred (Nirvair), beyond time (Akal Murat), self-existent and known by the Guru's grace (Gurprasad). God is both nirgun (beyond qualities) and sargun (present in creation).

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on the nature of god; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on the nature of god: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.2 God as creator

Key definitionKarta Purakh: God as the creator being.

Sikhs believe creation came from God's will (hukam). Because God is present in creation, the world is to be respected, which links belief to environmental and ethical responsibility.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on god as creator; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on god as creator: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.3 The nature of human life

Key definitionManmukh / gurmukh: a self-centred person versus a God-centred person.

Humans are born with the chance to realise God. Haumai (ego) and the five thieves (lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride) keep a person manmukh; following the Guru's teaching makes a person gurmukh.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on the nature of human life; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on the nature of human life: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.4 Sikh beliefs about life after death

Key definitionSamsara and mukti: the cycle of rebirth and liberation from it.

Karma shapes rebirth, but God's grace (nadar) is needed for mukti. Liberation is union with God, and Sikhs teach it can be reached while living (jivan mukt).

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on sikh beliefs about life after death; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on sikh beliefs about life after death: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.5 Purpose of life

Key definitionJivan mukt: one who is liberated while still alive.

The purpose of life is to become gurmukh and reach union with God through nam japna (remembering God), kirat karni (honest work) and vand chhakna (sharing).

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on purpose of life; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on purpose of life: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.6 The oneness of humanity

Key definitionEquality of all people because the same divine light is in everyone.

The Gurus rejected caste and gender hierarchy. The common surname Singh/Kaur and the langar where all sit together express this belief.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on the oneness of humanity; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on the oneness of humanity: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.7 Sewa

Key definitionSewa: selfless service done without reward.

Sewa can be physical (tan), mental (man) or material (dhan). It reduces haumai and is a route towards becoming gurmukh.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on sewa; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on sewa: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.
Concepts

1.1.8 Sangat

Key definitionSangat: the congregation or community of Sikhs.

Being in the sangat supports spiritual growth because members worship, sing kirtan and do sewa together; Sikh teaching values the company of the holy.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on sangat; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on sangat: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.

Diagrams

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing instead of explaining. Listing facts about the nature of god without saying why they matter earns AO1 only.
  2. Treating believers as one group. Questions often ask for divergent views; say that views differ and why.
  3. Vague definitions. Use the precise key term (e.g. "Ik Onkar") rather than everyday language.
  4. No source of wisdom in (c) answers. The 5-mark question requires a named source of wisdom and authority; without one the answer is capped.
  5. Unsupported judgements. A conclusion must follow from the arguments given, not appear from nowhere.
Examiner trapDo not move between topics: an answer on sangat must stay on that issue.

Example question stems

  • Outline three Sikh beliefs about the nature of god. (3)
  • Explain two reasons why god as creator is important to Sikhs. (4)
  • Explain two Sikh teachings about the nature of human life. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)
  • 'Sangat is the most important issue in this topic.' Evaluate this statement. (12)
Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationSource / scholarconnective

Explain question4 marks

Ik Onkar: there is one God, the opening of the Mool Mantar. this means that The Mool Mantar describes God as one, eternal truth (Sat Nam), creator (Karta Purakh), without fear (Nirbhau) and without hatred (Nirvair), beyond time (Akal Murat), self-existent and known by the Guru's grace (Gurprasad).

Karta Purakh: God as the creator being. this is because Sikhs believe creation came from God's will (hukam).

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective; no irrelevant material.
Evaluative answer: FOR → AGAINST → WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT12 marks

Statement: "The nature of God matters more than god as creator."

FOR: The Mool Mantar describes God as one, eternal truth (Sat Nam), creator (Karta Purakh), without fear (Nirbhau) and without hatred (Nirvair), beyond time (Akal Murat), self-existent and known by the Guru's grace (Gurprasad). God is both nirgun (beyond qualities) and sargun (present in creation). this shows that the nature of god shapes how Sikh believers understand everything else in the topic.

AGAINST: Sikhs believe creation came from God's will (hukam). Because God is present in creation, the world is to be respected, which links belief to environmental and ethical responsibility. However, without god as creator belief has little practical effect, which could lead to a faith that is only theoretical.

JUDGEMENT: Overall, the two are interdependent; therefore the statement is only partly convincing, because belief about the nature of god gives the reason for god as creator.

Why this is top levelLogical chains of reasoning on both sides, accurate knowledge, and a judgement that follows from the arguments. Add a named source of wisdom to strengthen AO1.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • "The stronger argument is … because it rests on a clearer source of authority."
  • "This view is persuasive to a believer, but less so to a non-religious thinker, because …"
  • "Although the nature of god is central, its importance depends on …"
  • "The weakness of this argument is that it assumes …"
  • "On balance, the evidence suggests …, so the statement is largely / partly / not convincing."
  • "Divergent views show there is no single answer; however, the most widely held view is …"
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn a precise definition for each of the 8 sub-topics on this page.
  2. Have at least one source of wisdom and authority ready for every sub-topic.
  3. Develop every point with a connective ("this means that", "therefore").
  4. Always show divergent views where they exist.
  5. End evaluative answers with a judgement that follows from your reasoning.
