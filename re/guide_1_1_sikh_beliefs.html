Concepts

1.1.1 The nature of God

Key definitionIk Onkar: there is one God, the opening of the Mool Mantar.

The Mool Mantar describes God as one, eternal truth (Sat Nam), creator (Karta Purakh), without fear (Nirbhau) and without hatred (Nirvair), beyond time (Akal Murat), self-existent and known by the Guru's grace (Gurprasad). God is both nirgun (beyond qualities) and sargun (present in creation). Because God is nirgun, God cannot be fully described; because God is sargun, God can be experienced in creation and in the Guru's word.

Common mistakeSaying Sikhs believe God has a human form. Sikhs reject avatars: God is never born and never dies (Ajuni).
Evaluation pointIs God better understood as nirgun or sargun? Sikh teaching holds both, so a strong answer argues they are complementary rather than choosing one.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the nature of god; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.2 God as creator

Key definitionKarta Purakh: God as the creator being.

Sikhs believe creation came from God's will (hukam). Because God is present in creation, the world is to be respected, which links belief to environmental and ethical responsibility. This means creation is not separate from God, so Sikhs treat the natural world and all people with respect.

Common mistakeConfusing creation by hukam with a six-day literal account; that is a Christian, not Sikh, framing.
Evaluation pointIf God is present in creation, does that make harming the environment a religious wrong? Most Sikhs would say yes.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about god as creator; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.3 The nature of human life

Key definitionManmukh / gurmukh: a self-centred person versus a God-centred person.

Humans are born with the chance to realise God. Haumai (ego) and the five thieves (lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride) keep a person manmukh; following the Guru's teaching makes a person gurmukh. Becoming gurmukh is a gradual process supported by the Guru's teaching, the sangat and daily discipline.

Common mistakeWriting that humans are born sinful. Sikhism teaches haumai is the obstacle, not original sin.
Evaluation pointIs haumai the biggest barrier to becoming gurmukh, or the five thieves? Argue that haumai is the root from which the thieves grow.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the nature of human life; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.4 Sikh beliefs about life after death

Key definitionSamsara and mukti: the cycle of rebirth and liberation from it.

Karma shapes rebirth, but God's grace (nadar) is needed for mukti. Liberation is union with God, and Sikhs teach it can be reached while living (jivan mukt). Rebirth may be as any life form; a human birth is seen as a precious opportunity to seek union with God.

Common mistakeSaying good karma alone gives mukti. Sikh teaching stresses God's grace (nadar) is also needed.
Evaluation pointIs it fair that grace, not just karma, decides liberation? Believers say it keeps humility; critics say it seems arbitrary.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about sikh beliefs about life after death; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.5 Purpose of life

Key definitionJivan mukt: one who is liberated while still alive.

The purpose of life is to become gurmukh and reach union with God through nam japna (remembering God), kirat karni (honest work) and vand chhakna (sharing). The three pillars balance devotion, work and generosity, so spiritual life is lived in the everyday world.

Common mistakeListing the three pillars without linking them to the goal of becoming gurmukh.
Evaluation pointIs honest work as spiritual as meditation? The Gurus' householder lives suggest yes.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about purpose of life; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.6 The oneness of humanity

Key definitionEquality of all people because the same divine light is in everyone.

The Gurus rejected caste and gender hierarchy. The common surname Singh/Kaur and the langar where all sit together express this belief. The Gurus also rejected the idea that any religion has a monopoly on God, which supports respect for people of other faiths.

Common mistakeClaiming equality is only a modern value. Guru Nanak rejected caste in the 15th–16th centuries.
Evaluation pointDoes equality in teaching match practice? Caste-based gurdwaras show a gap some Sikhs criticise.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about the oneness of humanity; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.7 Sewa

Key definitionSewa: selfless service done without reward.

Sewa can be physical (tan), mental (man) or material (dhan). It reduces haumai and is a route towards becoming gurmukh. Sewa is often seen in the gurdwara, for example cooking langar, cleaning, or looking after visitors' shoes.

Common mistakeTreating sewa as charity only. It is also tan (physical) and man (mental) service.
Evaluation pointIs sewa still sewa if done for praise? No: without humility it feeds haumai.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about sewa; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

1.1.8 Sangat

Key definitionSangat: the congregation or community of Sikhs.

Being in the sangat supports spiritual growth because members worship, sing kirtan and do sewa together; Sikh teaching values the company of the holy. Sikh teaching describes the company of the holy (sadh sangat) as helping a person turn away from haumai.

Common mistakeUsing sangat to mean the gurdwara building. Sangat is the people.
Evaluation pointCan a Sikh progress spiritually without the sangat? Teaching suggests the sangat is a strong support but individual nam japna also matters.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about sangat; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Sikh beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Calling the Guru Granth Sahib 'a holy book' only. Sikhs treat it as the living Guru; say so to show accurate understanding.
  2. Mixing Sikh and Hindu ideas of God. Sikhism rejects avatars and image worship.
  3. Forgetting grace (nadar) in mukti. Answers that only mention karma are incomplete.
  4. Vague 'Sikhs help people' for sewa. Name the three types of sewa and explain how sewa reduces haumai.
  5. No source of wisdom in (c). Use the Mool Mantar or a named Guru's teaching.

Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationconnective

Outline three Sikh beliefs about the nature of god. (3)3 marks
  1. Ik Onkar: there is one God, the opening of the Mool Mantar.
  2. Karta Purakh: God as the creator being.
  3. Manmukh / gurmukh: a self-centred person versus a God-centred person.
What makes this workThree separate, accurate points; no development is needed, which saves time for the 12-mark question.
Explain two Sikh beliefs about the nature of God. (4)4 marks

Sikhs believe God is one (Ik Onkar), which means that God cannot be divided or represented by images.

Sikhs also believe God is Nirbhau and Nirvair, without fear or hatred; this is because God is beyond human weaknesses, which shows that God is perfect.

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective that explains why it matters.
Explain two Sikh teachings about sewa. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)5 marks

Sewa is selfless service, and the Guru Granth Sahib teaches that service done without desire for reward is pleasing to God; this means that sewa must be humble to have value.

Sewa can be tan, man or dhan, which shows that every Sikh can serve whatever their wealth or ability.

Source of wisdom and authority: Guru Granth Sahib teaching on selfless service: exact verse wording to be supplied by teacher

What makes this workTwo developed points plus a named source of wisdom and authority that directly supports one of them. The exact source wording is marked for teacher confirmation.
Evaluate: 'Sewa is the most important part of being a Sikh.' (12)12 marks

FOR (1): Sewa reduces haumai and expresses the oneness of humanity; this means that it is a direct path towards becoming gurmukh.

FOR (2): The Gurus themselves served in the langar and cared for the sick; as a result sewa is part of following their example.

AGAINST (1): However, nam japna is the first pillar, and without remembering God sewa could become mere charity, which could lead to it feeding ego rather than removing it.

AGAINST (2): Others argue that belief in Ik Onkar comes first, because without it sewa has no spiritual purpose; this shows that belief underpins action.

WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT: Overall, sewa is essential but depends on nam japna for its meaning; therefore the statement is only partly true.

What makes this workTwo developed points on each side, each a chain of reasoning with highlighted connectives; the judgement weighs the sides and explains why one is stronger rather than simply repeating them. Adding a source of wisdom and authority on each side would strengthen it further.

Examples

Real-World Examples

Example: Langar during crisesGurdwaras across the UK ran free food services for vulnerable people during the COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020. This shows sewa and the oneness of humanity in practice, because food was given regardless of faith. Example: Khalsa AidKhalsa Aid, a UK-founded Sikh charity set up in 1999, provides disaster relief worldwide. Its motto 'recognise the whole human race as one' links directly to Sikh belief in the oneness of humanity. Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • Belief in Ik Onkar is the foundation of every other Sikh belief, because the oneness of God explains the oneness of humanity.
  • Sewa is the clearest expression of Sikh belief, since it turns teaching about haumai into daily action.
  • Karma alone cannot explain mukti, so the role of nadar (grace) shows that liberation depends on God as well as effort.
  • Sikh teaching on equality is clear, but the persistence of caste in some communities shows that practice does not always match belief.
  • The sangat strongly supports spiritual growth, yet it supports rather than replaces personal nam japna.
  • On balance, the three pillars are equally important because each one depends on the others.
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn the Mool Mantar descriptions of God by heart.
  2. Distinguish manmukh and gurmukh precisely.
  3. Mukti needs karma and grace.
  4. Name the three pillars: nam japna, kirat karni, vand chhakna.
  5. Three types of sewa: tan, man, dhan.
  6. Sangat means community, not building.
  7. Spend roughly a minute per mark, so leave most time for the 12-mark (d) question.
  8. In (c) answers, name the source precisely and explain how it supports the point.
  9. In (d) answers, give arguments on both sides, include a non-religious or divergent view where relevant, and finish with a justified conclusion.
