Concepts

3.4.1 Human rights

Key definitionHuman rights: rights belonging to every person.

Christians support human rights because humans are made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27). The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) sets out these rights.

Exam linkExpect a 3-mark outline or 4/5-mark explain question on human rights; for 5 marks add a source of wisdom and authority.
Evaluation pointWeigh how far believers agree on human rights: identify where views are shared, where they diverge, and which view is better supported by the reasons given.

3.4.2 Equality

Key definitionEquality: the state of being equal in status and rights.

Galatians 3:28 ('there is neither Jew nor Gentile...') is used to support equality.

Most Christians support religious freedom, linked to free will and respect for others.

3.4.4 Prejudice and discrimination

Key definitionPrejudice is an unfair attitude; discrimination is unfair action.

The parable of the Good Samaritan challenges prejudice; the Equality Act 2010 makes discrimination illegal in the UK.

Christians teach all are one in Christ; Martin Luther King Jr. drew on Christian teaching to campaign for civil rights.

Key definitionRacial discrimination: treating people unfairly because of race.

Christianity condemns racism, though historically some Christians used religion to justify it; churches have since apologised and campaigned against it.

Diagrams

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing instead of explaining. Listing facts about human rights without saying why they matter earns AO1 only.
  2. Treating believers as one group. Questions often ask for divergent views; say that views differ and why.
  3. Vague definitions. Use the precise key term (e.g. "Human rights") rather than everyday language.
  4. No source of wisdom in (c) answers. The 5-mark question requires a named source of wisdom and authority; without one the answer is capped.
  5. Unsupported judgements. A conclusion must follow from the arguments given, not appear from nowhere.
Examiner trapDo not move between topics: an answer on wealth and poverty must stay on that issue.

How It Comes Up

Example question stems

  • Outline three Christian beliefs about human rights. (3)
  • Explain two reasons why equality is important to Christians. (4)
  • Explain two Christian teachings about religious freedom. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)
  • 'Wealth and Poverty is the most important issue in this topic.' Evaluate this statement. (12)
Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationSource / scholarconnective

Explain question4 marks

Human rights: rights belonging to every person. this means that Christians support human rights because humans are made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27).

Equality: the state of being equal in status and rights. this is because Galatians 3:28 ('there is neither Jew nor Gentile...') is used to support equality..

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective; no irrelevant material.
Evaluative answer: FOR → AGAINST → WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT12 marks

Statement: "Human rights matters more than equality."

FOR: Christians support human rights because humans are made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27). The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) sets out these rights. this shows that human rights shapes how Christian believers understand everything else in the topic.

AGAINST: Galatians 3:28 ('there is neither Jew nor Gentile...') is used to support equality. However, without equality belief has little practical effect, which could lead to a faith that is only theoretical.

JUDGEMENT: Overall, the two are interdependent; therefore the statement is only partly convincing, because belief about human rights gives the reason for equality.

Why this is top levelLogical chains of reasoning on both sides, accurate knowledge, and a judgement that follows from the arguments. Add a named source of wisdom to strengthen AO1.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • "The stronger argument is … because it rests on a clearer source of authority."
  • "This view is persuasive to a believer, but less so to a non-religious thinker, because …"
  • "Although human rights is central, its importance depends on …"
  • "The weakness of this argument is that it assumes …"
  • "On balance, the evidence suggests …, so the statement is largely / partly / not convincing."
  • "Divergent views show there is no single answer; however, the most widely held view is …"
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn a precise definition for each of the 8 sub-topics on this page.
  2. Have at least one source of wisdom and authority ready for every sub-topic.
  3. Develop every point with a connective ("this means that", "therefore").
  4. Always show divergent views where they exist.
  5. End evaluative answers with a judgement that follows from your reasoning.
