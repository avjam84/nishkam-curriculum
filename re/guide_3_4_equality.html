Concepts

3.4.1 Human rights

Key definitionHuman rights: rights belonging to every person.

Christians support human rights because humans are made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27). The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) sets out these rights. Christian organisations campaign for rights such as freedom from poverty and persecution.

Common mistakeNo link between rights and imago Dei.
Evaluation pointDo Christians always support human rights? Most do, though some disagree over specific rights.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about human rights; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.2 Equality

Key definitionEquality: the state of being equal in status and rights.

Galatians 3:28 ('there is neither Jew nor Gentile...') is used to support equality. Christians differ on how equality applies to issues such as women's leadership in churches.

Common mistakeNo source quoted.
Evaluation pointIs equality achieved in churches? Debates on women's ordination suggest not fully.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about equality; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.3 Religious freedom

Key definitionReligious freedom: the right to hold and practise any belief.

Most Christians support religious freedom, linked to free will and respect for others. Christians may also defend their own freedom to worship where it is restricted.

Common mistakeAssuming Christians oppose other religions' freedom.
Evaluation pointCan religious freedom conflict with other rights? Sometimes, e.g. equality law.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about religious freedom; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.4 Prejudice and discrimination

Key definitionPrejudice is an unfair attitude; discrimination is unfair action.

The parable of the Good Samaritan challenges prejudice; the Equality Act 2010 makes discrimination illegal in the UK. Christians are called to love their neighbour, which excludes treating people unfairly.

Common mistakeConfusing prejudice and discrimination.
Evaluation pointIs prejudice harder to tackle than discrimination? Law can stop actions but not attitudes.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about prejudice and discrimination; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.5 Racial harmony

Key definitionRacial harmony: different races living together peacefully.

Christians teach all are one in Christ; Martin Luther King Jr. drew on Christian teaching to campaign for civil rights. Churches often bring together congregations from many ethnic backgrounds.

Common mistakeNo named example.
Evaluation pointHas Christianity promoted racial harmony? Yes, though with past failures.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about racial harmony; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.6 Racial Discrimination

Key definitionRacial discrimination: treating people unfairly because of race.

Christianity condemns racism, though historically some Christians used religion to justify it; churches have since apologised and campaigned against it. Churches have spoken out against racism in society and in their own history.

Common mistakeIgnoring historical failings.
Evaluation pointShould churches apologise for past racism? Many have.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about racial discrimination; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.7 Social Justice

Key definitionSocial justice: fair distribution of wealth, opportunity and rights.

Amos and Jesus' teaching urge justice for the poor; Catholic Social Teaching develops this. Many Christians support campaigns such as fair trade and debt relief.

Common mistakeVague definition.
Evaluation pointShould the church be political? Social justice campaigns suggest yes.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about social justice; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.
Concepts

3.4.8 Wealth and Poverty

Key definitionStewardship of wealth.

Wealth is not wrong but the love of money is (1 Timothy 6:10); Christians are called to help the poor (Matthew 25). Christians are encouraged to give money, time and skills to help others.

Common mistakeSaying Christians must give up all wealth.
Evaluation pointIs wealth compatible with faith? Yes if used well.
Exam linkA 3-mark (a) question could ask you to outline three points about wealth and poverty; a 4-mark (b) or 5-mark (c) question could ask you to explain two Christian beliefs or teachings about it, and for (c) you must add a source of wisdom and authority. In a 12-mark (d) answer, use this content as evidence for or against the statement.

Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Prejudice = discrimination. Prejudice is attitude; discrimination is action.
  2. No law knowledge. Equality Act 2010, UDHR 1948.
  3. One-sided on racism. Acknowledge historic failings.
  4. Wealth always wrong. Love of money is the issue.
  5. No source. Genesis 1:27, Galatians 3:28, Matthew 25.

Example question stems (written for this site, not Edexcel questions)

  • Outline three Christian beliefs about human rights. (3)
  • Explain two reasons why equality is important to Christians. (4)
  • Explain two Christian teachings about religious freedom. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority. (5)
  • 'Wealth and Poverty' – a 12-mark statement on this sub-topic, e.g. 'Religious people should fight for social justice.' Evaluate this statement. (12)
Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationconnective

Outline three Christian beliefs about human rights. (3)3 marks
  1. Human rights: rights belonging to every person.
  2. Equality: the state of being equal in status and rights.
  3. Religious freedom: the right to hold and practise any belief.
What makes this workThree separate, accurate points; no development is needed, which saves time for the 12-mark question.
Explain two Christian beliefs about prejudice and discrimination. (4)4 marks

Christians believe all people are created equal by God; this means that prejudice goes against God's will.

Jesus treated outcasts such as Samaritans with respect, which shows that Christians should treat everyone fairly.

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective that explains why it matters.
Explain two Christian teachings about wealth. (5)5 marks

The Bible teaches that 'the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil' (1 Timothy 6:10); this means that greed, not wealth itself, is wrong.

Jesus taught that helping the poor is helping him (Matthew 25), which shows that wealth should be shared.

Source of wisdom and authority: 1 Timothy 6:10 and Matthew 25: confirm references with teacher

What makes this workTwo developed points plus a named source of wisdom and authority that directly supports one of them. The exact source wording is marked for teacher confirmation.
Evaluate: 'Religious people should fight for social justice.' (12)12 marks

FOR (1): Amos and Jesus called for justice for the poor; this shows that faith requires action.

FOR (2): Martin Luther King used Christian teaching to campaign for civil rights; this means that faith has inspired real social change.

AGAINST (1): However, some argue religion should focus on spiritual matters, which could lead to churches staying out of politics.

AGAINST (2): Others argue social justice is the government's job, which could lead to the view that religious groups should focus on worship.

WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT: Overall, Christian teaching links faith and justice; therefore the statement is convincing.

What makes this workTwo developed points on each side, each a chain of reasoning with highlighted connectives; the judgement weighs the sides and explains why one is stronger rather than simply repeating them. Adding a source of wisdom and authority on each side would strengthen it further.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • Christian teaching strongly supports equality, but the history of Christian involvement in slavery shows that practice has not always followed it.
  • Law can reduce discrimination, though prejudice is harder to change because it is an attitude rather than an action.
  • Wealth is acceptable for Christians if it is used to help others rather than loved for its own sake.
  • Religious freedom matters, but it may conflict with equality law when beliefs lead to unequal treatment.
  • Christians are called to work for social justice, because Jesus identified himself with the poor in Matthew 25.
  • Overall, Christian teaching on equality is clear, and the main challenge is putting it into practice.
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Imago Dei: Genesis 1:27.
  2. Galatians 3:28.
  3. UDHR 1948; Equality Act 2010.
  4. Prejudice vs discrimination.
  5. MLK and Tutu.
  6. 1 Timothy 6:10.
  7. Spend roughly a minute per mark, so leave most time for the 12-mark (d) question.
  8. In (c) answers, name the source precisely and explain how it supports the point.
  9. In (d) answers, give arguments on both sides, include a non-religious or divergent view where relevant, and finish with a justified conclusion.
