Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing instead of explaining. Listing facts about an introduction to ethical theories, practical ethics and the relationship between religion and morality without saying why they matter earns AO1 only.
  2. Treating believers as one group. Questions often ask for divergent views; say that views differ and why.
  3. Vague definitions. Use the precise key term (e.g. "Normative ethics") rather than everyday language.
  4. Narrating scholars without evaluating them. A level essays reward critical analysis, not summaries of who said what.
  5. Unsupported judgements. A conclusion must follow from the arguments given, not appear from nowhere.
Examiner trapDo not move between topics: an answer on medical ethics with a focus on beginning and end of life debates must stay on that issue.

Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationSource / scholarconnective

Explain-style answerTariff TBC

Normative ethics: theories of how we should act. this means that Students compare teleological and deontological approaches and apply them to issues..

Environmental ethics: moral duties to the natural world. this is because Stewardship and dominion versus deep ecology..

What makes this workTwo distinct points, each developed with a connective; no irrelevant material.
Evaluative answer: FOR → AGAINST → WEIGHTED JUDGEMENTTariff TBC

Statement: "An Introduction to Ethical theories, practical ethics and the relationship between religion and morality matters more than environmental issues."

FOR: Students compare teleological and deontological approaches and apply them to issues. this shows that an introduction to ethical theories, practical ethics and the relationship between religion and morality shapes how scholarly believers understand everything else in the topic.

AGAINST: Stewardship and dominion versus deep ecology. However, without environmental issues belief has little practical effect, which could lead to a faith that is only theoretical.

JUDGEMENT: Overall, the two are interdependent; therefore the statement is only partly convincing, because belief about an introduction to ethical theories, practical ethics and the relationship between religion and morality gives the reason for environmental issues.

Why this is top levelLogical chains of reasoning on both sides, accurate knowledge, and a judgement that follows from the arguments.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • "The stronger argument is … because it rests on a clearer source of authority."
  • "This view is persuasive to a believer, but less so to a non-religious thinker, because …"
  • "Although an introduction to ethical theories, practical ethics and the relationship between religion and morality is central, its importance depends on …"
  • "The weakness of this argument is that it assumes …"
  • "On balance, the evidence suggests …, so the statement is largely / partly / not convincing."
  • "Divergent views show there is no single answer; however, the most widely held view is …"
Reminders

Exam Reminders

  1. Learn a precise definition for each of the 13 sub-topics on this page.
  2. Know at least one scholar or argument for and against each sub-topic.
  3. Develop every point with a connective ("this means that", "therefore").
  4. Always show divergent views where they exist.
  5. End evaluative answers with a judgement that follows from your reasoning.
