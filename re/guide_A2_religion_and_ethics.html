Traps

Common Mistakes

  1. Describing a theory without applying it. Questions on practical ethics expect theories applied to the issue.
  2. Confusing act and rule utilitarianism. They give different answers to the same case, so mixing them weakens analysis.
  3. Misstating Kant. The categorical imperative concerns universalisability and treating people as ends, not consequences.
  4. Confusing meta-ethics with normative ethics. Meta-ethics asks what 'good' means, not what we should do.
  5. No judgement. An essay must reach a conclusion that follows from its argument.

Answers

Colour-Coded Model Answers

Key: AO1 knowledgeAO2 analysis / evaluationconnective

Explain situation ethics.Tariff TBC

Fletcher argued the only intrinsic good is agape; this means that the right act is the most loving in each situation.

He used four working principles, including pragmatism and personalism, which shows the theory focuses on people over rules.

What makes this workAccurate, precise knowledge (AO1) with each idea explained and linked to the scholar or teaching it comes from.
Evaluate: 'Utilitarianism is the best approach to medical ethics.'Tariff TBC

FOR (1): It weighs outcomes; this is because medical decisions affect wellbeing.

FOR (2): It treats each patient's wellbeing as equal, which shows fairness in resource decisions.

AGAINST (1): However, it may justify sacrificing individuals, which could lead to injustice.

AGAINST (2): Rule utilitarians respond with rules protecting rights, however this may collapse back into act utilitarianism.

WEIGHTED JUDGEMENT: Overall, it is useful but needs rights-based limits.

What makes this workTwo developed points on each side, each a chain of reasoning with highlighted connectives; the judgement weighs the sides and explains why one is stronger rather than simply repeating them.

Evaluation

Judgement Phrases for Evaluation

  • Utilitarianism is practical in medical ethics, but it risks justifying harm to individuals for the sake of the majority.
  • Kant's theory gives clear, universal rules, yet its rigidity seems to produce harsh results in cases such as lying to a murderer.
  • Natural law remains relevant because it is grounded in human nature and purpose, which many people still recognise.
  • Situation ethics is flexible, but agape alone may be too vague to guide difficult decisions.
  • Virtue ethics focuses on character, which is valuable but gives less guidance on specific dilemmas.
  • Overall, no single theory is adequate on its own, so many thinkers combine insights from several.

  8. Evaluate every scholar you mention rather than just describing them.
  9. Keep the judgement consistent with the argument from the first paragraph.
